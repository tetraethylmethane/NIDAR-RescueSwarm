THE SEALED BAY -- what happens if you trust propwash
==============================================================================
  Bay 150 x 100 x 60 mm, surface 0.060 m2, load 18 W (companion + camera)

                   condition       h   steady dT   bay air
           sealed, still air      7       42.9 K     77.9 C
      forced air, modest fan     30       10.0 K     45.0 C

  Time constant, sealed: 429 s. Over a 285 s setup the bay
  reaches 49% of that steady rise = 21 K, so about 56 C of bay air
  by launch -- BEFORE the mission starts, and before any propwash.

  That is bay AIR. Silicon junction sits above it. A companion that
  throttles during the search is a detection-rate problem, and throttling
  is exactly what this produces: it arrives late, under load, with no
  obvious cause on the ground station.
==============================================================================
THE FAN IS TRIVIALLY SMALL -- there is no reason not to fit it
==============================================================================
  Flow needed to carry the heat away at a given air temperature rise:

     air dT    mass flow      volume     CFM
        5 K       3.58 g/s      3.09 L/s    6.5
       10 K       1.79 g/s      1.54 L/s    3.3
       20 K       0.90 g/s      0.77 L/s    1.6

  A 40 mm fan moves several CFM on a fraction of a watt. The cooling
  requirement is not hard; REMEMBERING TO POWER IT FROM THE MAIN BUS AT
  POWER-ON is the requirement. A fan gated on arming, or on flight mode,
  is off for the entire window this analysis is about.

THE COUPLING NOBODY MODELS -- compute heat moves the camera
==============================================================================
  boresight_budget.py budgets 0.153 deg (0.21 mm over an 80 mm spacing)
  for boresight, and shows a 100 mm aluminium bracket growing 0.066 mm
  over a 30 K swing -- a third of the allowance.

  That analysis assumed AMBIENT swing, dawn to midday. If the camera
  bracket is bolted to, or shares structure with, the compute bay, it
  also sees the bay's rise:

        sealed bay: +42.9 K at the bracket -> 0.094 mm -> 0.068 deg -> 0.07 m at 60 m
    fan-cooled bay: +10.0 K at the bracket -> 0.022 mm -> 0.016 deg -> 0.02 m at 60 m

  BE HONEST ABOUT THE SIZE OF THIS. 0.07 m added in quadrature to the
  0.884 m case-C geotag gives 0.887 m. It is nearly nothing on its own,
  and it does not justify contorting the layout.

  Two reasons it is still worth a line on the drawing. It is SYSTEMATIC,
  so it stacks with the boresight residual rather than averaging out
  across frames. And unlike the ambient swing it tracks COMPUTE LOAD, so
  it moves when the detector starts working -- during the search, while
  geotagging. Calibrating on a cold aircraft and flying a hot one is the
  kind of error that shows up as an unexplained bias in P7 and costs
  days to find.

  CAD consequence, and it is free at design time: do not bolt the camera
  bracket to the compute bay. That is the whole ask -- not a heat pipe,
  not an isolator, just do not share the structure.
==============================================================================
THE BATTERY IS THE OPPOSITE PROBLEM
==============================================================================
  battery_failsafe.py puts pack I2R at 54 W in hover -- three times the
  companion -- and an adiabatic 19.7 K over the 7.7 min mission.

  But on the ground at 55 W avionics draw the pack contributes almost
  nothing, and in flight it has full propwash over it.

  So the two heat sources need opposite things:
     compute  hot on the GROUND, needs a fan, props stopped
     battery  hot in FLIGHT, has propwash, needs a path through it

  A single 'propwash cools everything' layout satisfies the battery and
  fails the compute bay in the one window where the aircraft is powered
  and stationary.
==============================================================================
WHAT CAD HAS TO DELIVER
==============================================================================
  1. Fan powered from the main bus AT POWER-ON. Not on arming, not on
     mode. SYS-53, and the reason is the setup window.
  2. Inlet and outlet that stay clear when the aircraft is on the ground
     and -- if arms fold -- when it is folded. A blocked duct fails in
     the configuration it spends the setup window in.
  3. Camera bracket thermally isolated from the compute bay.
  4. Battery airflow path through propwash, separate from the compute
     bay so a hot pack does not preheat the companion.
  5. Filtration is a real trade in a flood zone: a filter that halves
     the flow doubles the rise. Decide it deliberately rather than
     discovering it -- and note that ingress protection is a real
     requirement in standing water, not an optional nicety.
