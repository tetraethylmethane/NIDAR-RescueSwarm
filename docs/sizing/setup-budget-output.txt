FOLDING ARMS  -  what deployment time does the window afford?
==============================================================================
  Case D (recommended) with per-aircraft deploy-and-verify time added.
  Read the CALIBRATED column: case D calibrated sits at 285 s against
  a 300 s window, so there is ~15 s of real margin to spend.

   per aircraft   launch   margin  calibrated   verdict
     fixed arms     185s    +115s        285s        OK
           10 s     185s    +115s        285s        OK
           15 s     185s    +115s        285s        OK
           20 s     185s    +115s        285s        OK
           22 s     185s    +115s        285s        OK
           25 s     190s    +110s        290s        OK
           28 s     199s    +101s        299s        OK
           30 s     205s     +95s        305s      OVER
           45 s     230s     +70s        330s      OVER

  THE RESULT IS NOT THE ONE THE ARGUMENT EXPECTED. Deployment is FREE
  up to about 20 s per aircraft: launch stays at 185 s. It hides in
  crew slack, because the critical path in this window is not the
  crew at all -- it is the 75 s companion boot, then mesh association
  and mission-file parse. Two crew have idle time during that, and
  unfolding arms is exactly the kind of work that fits into it.

  Free to 22 s. At 25-28 s it starts pushing pre-arm and the
  calibrated case lands on 290-299 s -- inside 300 s arithmetically,
  but 1 s is not margin, it is a coin toss. Over at 30 s.

  So the honest budget is 22 s per aircraft, and 28 s is the point
  of no return. Deploy, lock and VERIFY in 22 s is achievable with
  quick-release clamps and a lock indicator you can see from a metre
  away. It is not achievable with bolts, and it is not achievable if
  the crew have to think about it.

  The launch box does not force the decision either: three aircraft
  at 1046 mm square (20 in props) sit side by side in 3138 mm, inside
  the 3.66 m box, unfolded. Folding buys TRANSPORT volume only --
  roughly 944 mm square down to ~450 mm with arms back.

  What remains against folding is structural, not schedule: a fold is
  a designed break point, and any fold between the camera and the
  GNSS antennas is a lever arm that changes on every unpack, which
  invalidates SYS-48. Keep folds strictly outboard of that core and
  the boresight objection goes away too.

==============================================================================
WHY THE ABSOLUTE BASE POSITION BARELY MATTERS FOR DELIVERY
==============================================================================
  The base's absolute error shifts every aircraft position by the same
  vector. The survivor is geotagged from a drone carrying that shift,
  and the delivery drone flies to that coordinate carrying the SAME
  shift -- so the kit lands on the true survivor. The error cancels.

  It does NOT cancel if the 250-point geotag score is judged by
  comparing our DISPLAYED coordinates against surveyed truth. That is
  an open question and the reason to still want a good absolute fix.

  Consequence: a long survey-in buys accuracy we mostly do not need.
  Trading 90 s of survey-in for ~1-2 m of common-mode bias is a good
  trade when the window has 15 s of margin.
