==============================================================================
  The programme total is the wrong number to quote a customer, because
  most of it is one-off. The marginal cost is what scales.

  marginal aircraft, taxes included   INR     334,029  (  3.34 L)
  a 3-aircraft operational fleet      INR   1,002,086  ( 10.02 L)
  + ground segment (one per fleet)    INR     458,194  (  4.58 L)
  DEPLOYABLE SYSTEM, cost basis      INR   1,460,280  ( 14.60 L)

  Cost basis, not price. Margin, warranty, training, support and
  certification are commercial decisions, not BOM arithmetic.

RECURRING COST  -  what a deployment consumes
==============================================================================
  payload and kits, as bought         INR      40,880  (  0.41 L)

  Per-mission consumption depends on three things this repo does not
  know yet, and inventing them would be worse than leaving them open:

    * kit refill cost -- the BOM buys competition kits, not relief kits
    * battery cycle life -- 21700 packs at this C-rate, in Indian heat
    * crew and transport per deployment

  What IS known: the fleet carries 3 x 4 = 12 kits per sortie, hover
  endurance is 15.3 min against a 7.7 min design mission, and the pack
  is 292 Wh, so a sortie is a few rupees of electricity and a pack cycle.
  The consumable that matters is kits, not energy.

INDIGENISATION  -  a scored differentiator, not a slogan
==============================================================================
  Value-weighted Indian content across the priced BOM: 67.9%

  programme as costed (67.9% Indian)    INR   2,873,880  ( 28.74 L)
  the same BOM fully imported           INR   3,274,825  ( 32.75 L)
  saved by sourcing in India            INR     400,945  (  4.01 L)   12%

  This is worth stating precisely because the audience is MeitY and the
  Drone Federation of India, and because it is measurable rather than
  asserted -- every line in the BOM carries a declared Indian fraction.

  It also buys schedule, which matters more than the money here: the
  BOM's own note puts imports at 4-6 weeks against 2-3 weeks domestic,
  inside a programme whose binding constraint is an 8-week flight window.

WHAT THIS MODULE WILL NOT DO
==============================================================================
  These are the pitch's remaining numbers, and they need real sources.
  Each is a question for the team, not a gap to be filled with a guess:

    market size      how many flood-response units, at what refresh rate
    willingness to   what NDRF/SDRF or a state disaster authority
      pay            actually pays for comparable capability
    alternative      cost per flight hour of the helicopter or boat
      cost           search this displaces
    adoption         DGCA category, BVLOS status, and what a state
                     procurement cycle takes

  A pitch that derives its cost from a real BOM and admits which
  numbers are unsourced is stronger than one that is confident
  throughout, because the first can be checked.
