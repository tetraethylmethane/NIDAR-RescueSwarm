HOW MUCH BORESIGHT ERROR IS THE BUDGET BUYING?
==============================================================================
  The 0.16 m case-C allocation corresponds to:
     0.153 deg at 60 m AGL   (2.67 mrad)
     0.229 deg at 40 m AGL   (4.00 mrad)

      angle     @40 m     @60 m   note
      0.05d     0.03m     0.05m   
      0.10d     0.07m     0.10m   
      0.15d     0.10m     0.16m   the budgeted allocation at 60 m
      0.25d     0.17m     0.26m   
      0.50d     0.35m     0.52m   
      1.00d     0.70m     1.05m   a mount that shifted, or a skipped calibration
      2.00d     1.40m     2.10m   
==============================================================================
BUT CHECK ITS LEVERAGE BEFORE SPENDING MONEY ON IT
==============================================================================
  Case C geotag RSS with every term at budget: 0.884 m

                        term   value   share of variance
                  unmodelled   0.70m              62.7%
    target extent / centroid   0.50m              32.0%
          boresight residual   0.16m               3.3%
       GNSS-camera lever arm   0.10m               1.3%
                    attitude   0.07m               0.6%
              pixel centroid   0.02m               0.1%

  Boresight at budget is a SMALL term -- a few percent of variance. The
  0.70 m unmodelled allowance and the 0.50 m target-extent term dominate
  case C between them.

  boresight doubles to 0.32 m                geotag 0.93 m  (+0.04 m)
  boresight quadruples to 0.64 m             geotag 1.08 m  (+0.20 m)
  boresight becomes 1.05 m = 1 deg at 60 m   geotag 1.37 m  (+0.48 m)

  THE POINT IS NOT OPTIMISATION, IT IS FAILURE MODE.
  Tightening boresight below its 0.16 m allocation buys almost nothing.
  Letting it go to a degree -- one shifted mount, one skipped
  calibration -- makes it the largest single term in the stack and
  costs about 0.5 m of geotag, silently, with nothing on the screen to
  say so. Design the mount to HOLD calibration, not to be precise.
==============================================================================
WHAT THAT MEANS IN MILLIMETRES
==============================================================================
  A mount rotates when one side moves relative to the other. Over a
  fastener spacing L, an angular error a needs a differential of
  L * tan(a):

    spacing    0.15 deg    0.50 deg    1.0 deg
       40mm      0.105mm      0.349mm     0.698mm
       60mm      0.157mm      0.524mm     1.047mm
       80mm      0.209mm      0.698mm     1.396mm
      120mm      0.314mm      1.047mm     2.095mm

  Read the 0.15 deg column. On an 80 mm fastener spacing the budget is
  0.21 mm of differential movement -- TOTAL, for the life of the
  airframe, through transport, vibration and every landing.

  A clearance-hole bolted joint does not hold 0.2 mm. Standard M3
  clearance is 0.2-0.4 mm of radial slop on its own, and it only takes
  one hard landing to take it up. That is the argument for dowel pins
  or a bonded joint, and it is a number rather than a preference.
==============================================================================
THERMAL -- the one nobody checks
==============================================================================
  Carbon fibre is ~1 ppm/K. Aluminium is ~23 ppm/K. An aluminium camera
  bracket bolted flat to a CF plate is a bimetallic strip.

    bracket    dT  differential   as angle over 80 mm
       60mm   15K        0.020mm                0.014d
       60mm   30K        0.040mm                0.028d
      100mm   15K        0.033mm                0.024d
      100mm   30K        0.066mm                0.047d

  A 100 mm aluminium bracket over a 30 K day-night swing moves 0.066 mm,
  which is a third of the whole 0.15 deg allowance if it turns into
  rotation. It will not all become rotation in a well-designed joint --
  but it will in an over-constrained one.

  Calibrate at the temperature you fly at, or make the bracket CF, or
  constrain it kinematically so growth translates instead of rotating.
==============================================================================
LEVER ARM -- cheap, so just do it properly
==============================================================================
  The GNSS-camera lever arm is budgeted at 0.10 m and is systematic.
  Measured to 1 cm instead: geotag 0.878 m against 0.884 m

  Worth 0.005 m. Almost nothing -- and that is the useful finding,
  because it means the lever arm does NOT justify design compromises.
  What it does justify is a MEASURABLE DATUM: a defined feature you can
  put callipers on between the antenna phase centre and the camera
  mounting face. The cost of that is one dimension on a drawing, and
  without it the 0.10 m is a guess rather than a budget line.
==============================================================================
WHAT CAD HAS TO DELIVER
==============================================================================
  1. Camera, IMU and both GNSS antenna mounts on ONE rigid core. No
     folding joint anywhere in that load path -- a fold re-datums the
     lever arm on every unpack and invalidates SYS-48.
  2. Camera located by DOWELS or bonded, not by bolt friction. Budget
     is 0.21 mm of differential over an 80 mm spacing, for life.
  3. A measurable datum between antenna phase centre and camera face.
  4. Kinematic or CF bracket so thermal growth translates, not rotates.
  5. A calibration target arrangement that can be re-flown in the field,
     because the honest assumption is that boresight WILL move and the
     question is whether anyone finds out.
