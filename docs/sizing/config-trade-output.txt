  Every config lands at exactly 15.5 min hover / 2.00x mission, because the
  2x-endurance rule -- not the energy rule -- is what binds. Pack size is
  therefore proportional to hover power, and a power penalty compounds:
  more battery -> more mass -> more power. That is the coaxial story.

  COAXIAL VERDICT: +59 to +147% hover power and +33 to +61% fleet mass,
  cutting the 23% mass margin to -24%. It buys NO footprint back --
  stacking rotors does not shorten the arms, so the X8 footprint equals
  the quad it replaces. Coaxial pays only when rotors must shrink to fit
  a box; the quad already fits 3 per row. Rejected at every kappa.

  v_i        hover induced velocity, sqrt(DL*g/2rho)
  VRS        vertical descent band where vortex ring state develops.
             Onset is conventionally taken at 0.25-0.5 v_i, worst near 0.7-1.0.
  desc@2.5   the model's 2.5 m/s descent rate as a fraction of v_i
  gust dT/W  vertical gust sensitivity = 1/v_i exactly (from T = 2 rho A v_i^2,
             dT/dV at V=0 is 2 rho A v_i, normalised by W). A 1 m/s vertical
             gust perturbs thrust by this fraction of weight, open loop.
             LOW disk loading = MORE gust sensitive. Not a virtue here.
  J_rotor    total rotor inertia proxy, sum(m_prop * R^2), vs current quad.
             Higher = slower thrust response = lower attitude bandwidth.
             PROXY ONLY. Response time scales steeply with diameter
             (tau ~ D^3.5 at fixed aircraft power); needs bench measurement.

  Nulled-groundspeed descent is near-vertical, which is exactly the
  condition VRS needs. Two fixes, both nearly free against a 74% time margin:
    (a) descend at <= 1.25 m/s (0.25 v_i) -- costs +21.6 s per drop, or
        ~+72 s per aircraft over 3.3 drops, on a 7.7 of 30 min mission;
    (b) keep horizontal speed >= v_i (6.5 m/s) through the descent and
        decelerate to hover at the 6 m drop altitude -- costs nothing.
  (b) is preferred: it also cuts exposure time in the gust-sensitive regime.
