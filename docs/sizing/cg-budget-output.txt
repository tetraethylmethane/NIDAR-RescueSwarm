==============================================================================
STATIC CG -- what a placement error costs
==============================================================================
  A build tolerance on where the pack sits is a permanent trim offset.

    pack offset   CG shift     trim   note
           0 mm       0.0 mm    0.0%   
           5 mm       1.1 mm    0.6%   
          10 mm       2.3 mm    1.2%   a plausible build tolerance
          20 mm       4.6 mm    2.4%   
          40 mm       9.1 mm    4.8%   a pack seated the wrong way round in its tray

  The pack is 22.8 % of MTOW, so its offset transfers to the CG at
  roughly 0.23 mm per mm. That is forgiving -- but it is also why the
  tray must be a REPEATABLE location, not a strap: a pack that seats
  differently each flight re-trims the aircraft each flight.
==============================================================================
KIT RELEASE -- the CG walks during the mission
==============================================================================
  800 g of the 6360 g leaves, one kit at a time, at whatever survivor
  the mission finds. The airframe cannot choose when; it can only
  choose the LAYOUT and the ORDER.

  2x2 around the CG (+/-100, +/-55 mm)
     best release order  [0, 1, 2, 3]   peak CG offset   4.0 mm  trim 2.1%
     worst order         [0, 2, 1, 3]   peak CG offset   6.7 mm  trim 3.5%

  row of 4 fore-aft (+/-165, +/-55 mm)
     best release order  [1, 3, 0, 2]   peak CG offset   3.7 mm  trim 1.9%
     worst order         [0, 1, 2, 3]   peak CG offset   7.4 mm  trim 3.9%

  row of 4 across (+/-165, +/-55 mm)
     best release order  [1, 3, 0, 2]   peak CG offset   3.7 mm  trim 1.9%
     worst order         [0, 1, 2, 3]   peak CG offset   7.4 mm  trim 3.9%

  READ THAT TABLE AGAIN BEFORE CONSTRAINING THE CAD.

  Layout barely matters: best-order peak excursion is 3.7 mm for both
  rows and 4.0 mm for the 2x2. The row is marginally BETTER, which is
  the opposite of the tidy answer, because a row lets you alternate
  about the centre while a 2x2 forces a corner to be last.

  ORDER matters about twice as much as layout: 3.7 mm against 7.4 mm
  on the same row, purely from which kit goes first.

  And every number here is small -- under 4 % of hover thrust in the
  worst case. This is NOT a stability problem and it should NOT be
  used to constrain the magazine geometry. Lay the magazine out for
  PACKAGING and drop-path clearance; the CG will follow.

  What it IS: a repeatability nudge. Each release steps the trim and
  the autopilot needs a moment to settle before the next drop is
  accurate. That makes release ORDER an autonomy decision worth making
  deliberately -- alternate about the centre rather than working along
  the magazine -- and it costs nothing to get right.
==============================================================================
VERTICAL CG -- the one that is easy to get wrong
==============================================================================
  The pack is the heaviest item and the most tempting to hang low for
  'stability'. On a multirotor that is a myth worth naming: the rotors
  are the control effectors, and a CG far BELOW the rotor plane makes
  the airframe a pendulum that the controller has to fight, coupling
  attitude into translation exactly during the slow, precise hover a
  delivery needs.

  Keep the pack CG as close to the rotor plane as the geometry allows.
  The magazine hangs below by necessity -- kits have to fall -- so the
  pack should not add to that.

  Concrete consequence for the bay: the 6S3P block is 63 mm deep bare
  against 42 mm for the superseded 6S2P. Those 21 mm should be taken
  by making the tray WIDER or LONGER, not by dropping it 21 mm lower.
==============================================================================
WHAT THIS MEANS FOR CAD
==============================================================================
  1. Side-loading battery tray with a captive latch and a hard stop.
     Repeatability beats adjustability: the same pack in the same place
     every flight is what keeps trim constant. It also keeps the swap
     off the critical path -- see setup_budget.py -- and avoids
     disturbing the parachute mount above or the magazine below.
  2. Magazine geometry: choose it for PACKAGING and drop-path clearance,
     not for CG. The sweep above says layout is worth 0.3 mm and order
     is worth 3.7 mm, and all of it is under 4 % of hover thrust.
     Centre the group on the CG and stop optimising there.
  2a. Release ORDER is the part worth deciding: alternate about the
     centre instead of working along the magazine. That is autonomy's
     job, not CAD's, and it halves the peak excursion for free.
  3. Pack CG in the rotor plane, not slung under it.
  4. Current shunt for BATT_MONITOR=4 must be in the pack path and
     reachable: BATT_RESISTANCE has to be MEASURED on the real pack
     before first flight -- see battery_failsafe.py, where an unset
     resistance had the low-battery failsafe firing at 53 % SoC.
